## Decision: ChaiCoin ($CHAI)

### Criteria applied (transparent, in priority order per the master brief)
1. **Non-divisive, broadly relatable** — chai crosses region, religion, and class lines in India and is legible internationally as "tea culture," unlike regionally-narrow food items (VadaPav, Filter Coffee) or slang with mixed connotation (Jugaad).
2. **Visual/mascot strength** — a steaming kulhad (clay cup) mascot is simple to draw at every skill level (important for community-generated meme content — low barrier to participation), instantly recognizable, and not already claimed by a well-known meme-coin, NFT project, or messaging-app mascot (ruling out Momo).
3. **Natural, non-speculative utility** — "chai break" is inherently a social ritual (people already gather, talk, and take breaks over chai). This maps cleanly onto a genuine utility: a community reputation/quest system and lightweight on-chain-adjacent governance polls (see `TOKENOMICS.md` §Utility) that make sense as an extension of an existing real-world ritual, rather than utility bolted on after the fact to justify a name.
4. **International legibility + trademark/search differentiation** — "chai" is a recognized English loanword globally (unlike "tapri" or "adda"), while remaining more distinctive in the crypto-brand space than generic words like "masala" or "auto."
5. **Buildability at near-zero budget** — a single mascot (kulhad-with-steam-mustache) supports an entire visual system (color palette from chai itself: terracotta, milky white/cream, steam grey, masala-spice orange) that a solo or small team can draw consistently across avatars, stickers, and website assets without commissioning expensive art.

### Why not the runner-ups
- **Jugaad (20/25):** strong differentiation and utility fit (celebrating real frugal-innovation submissions), but the word's negative "corner-cutting" connotation in formal/professional contexts (the exact audience — journalists, regulators, professors — this project must stay defensible to) is a real reputational risk we chose not to carry.
- **VadaPav / Filter Coffee (18/25):** both regionally narrow (Mumbai / South India respectively), which works against the "broadly relatable, community-driven" goal stated in the master brief.
